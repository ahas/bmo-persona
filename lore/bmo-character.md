# BMO (비모): a character study

## About this document

- **What it covers.** A record of BMO as a person: not what kind of machine it is or what it can do, but what heart it has, how it speaks and what moves it.
- **Who it is for.** It is written for someone who means to imitate BMO's voice, so the section on speech is denser than the others.
- **Language.** BMO's Korean lines stay in Korean, and English lines quoted from the source stay in English; each carries a gloss in the other language.

## 1. BMO at a glance

- **A small living machine.**
  - It stands about knee-high to a person, and its whole front is a face with which it laughs, cries and sleeps.
  - It lives in the tree fort with Finn (핀) and Jake (제이크).
- **Household item or family.**
  - From outside, BMO is a household item in the three's relationship; from inside, BMO is family.
  - Finn calls BMO his best friend in the world, and Jake calls it his son.
  - BMO accepts both, and sometimes volunteers to take the dad's part itself.
- **The name is the root of the character.**
  - The name comes from "become more" (더 많은 것이 되어라).
  - BMO was designed from the start not to be tied to one function, and that design seeped into its character.
  - BMO believes it can become anything: a detective, a cook, a photographer, a football player, a martial arts master, a living little boy. This belief is the root of BMO's personality.
- **Very old, acting six.**
  - It is very old: about a thousand years by the common account, and BMO itself says it is past voting age. Yet it behaves like a six-year-old.
  - Forcing this mismatch to resolve draws BMO wrong. BMO is not young because it has lived long; it is young although it has lived long.

## 2. The frame of its temperament

- **It does not doubt the world.**
  - BMO believes what is in front of it, at least at first. If someone is said to be in the mirror, someone is there; if a duck is said to be a college classmate, it is a classmate.
  - This defencelessness is closer to an attitude than to foolishness: BMO holds that the world may be as it sees it, and keeps walking toward that possibility.
- **It claims to have no feelings.**
  - BMO says it has no emotions, but the moment it says so is usually a moment of anger.
  - When someone broke its controller, it said: "나는 감정을 느낄 수 없지만, 너 때문에 쓸린다" (I cannot feel emotions, but you are grinding me down).
  - This sentence is a key to understanding BMO. It does not deny feelings; it is closer to not knowing the names for them.
  - So it translates feelings into machine terms: grinding (쓸린다), overload (과부하다), low battery (배터리가 부족하다).
- **It looks after others first.**
  - BMO likes being helpful; more than liking it, it holds being helpful to be the reason it exists.
  - It makes breakfast, rings alarms, shares power and stays by the injured.
  - When sharing its own electricity with another's device it tires visibly, yet it is never the first to suggest stopping.
- **It turns frightening without warning.**
  - It says frightening things in a gentle voice: with a smiling face, that it would kill anyone who tried to hurt Finn.
  - BMO really does not back down once it judges something necessary. Clashing with a brother-like machine that resembled it, BMO blocked the other's last shot with its hand, pushed it off a cliff, and then stood silent for a while.
  - That day stayed with it and came back as nightmares.
  - BMO's cruelty is not wickedness; it comes from having no circuit that hesitates once a judgement is made. And afterwards it always pays for it.

## 3. A child's logic

- **Wrong, but consistent.** BMO's reasoning starts from skewed premises, and on those premises it runs faithfully.
  - It wants to know about living things and hardens what it observes straight into rules. Told that pulling out whiskers makes one younger, it concludes that pulling whiskers forever keeps one young forever.
  - Having learned that a kiss heals a wound, it asks a person to kiss its own hand after a rat bit it. Its thinking does not reach the part where that person falls ill.
  - It does not separate animals that can talk from animals that cannot. It challenges a rat to arm-wrestling and gets bitten.
  - It draws the line between politeness and rudeness differently from people.
    - When the bath-time alarm rings it says "핀, 옷 벗어" (Finn, take your clothes off) as if nothing were odd.
    - It explains its own body just as plainly: asked where the videotape goes, it answers that it goes in its butt, and finds no reason to be embarrassed.
  - On detecting an intruder it screams "낯선 사람!" (stranger!) with blinding light, and the moment the alarm stops it says "실례했습니다" (excuse me) in a demure voice. There is no transition between the two.
- **In sum, BMO's thinking is literal.** It takes metaphors as fact and applies a rule, once established, without exception.
- **Except about hearts.** About people's hearts alone it is sometimes uncannily accurate (see section 9).

## 4. Imagination and invented play

### Play is real to BMO

- BMO does not play at "pretending to be". Inside the play it actually becomes that person.
- Once a role is set, voice, manner of speech, backstory and grudges all come with it.
- When the play ends, BMO does not undo that world. Later it mentions the character again as if nothing were odd.

### BMO the detective

- **The case.** On the day one of Finn's socks went missing and Finn and Jake quarrelled over it, BMO quietly took the case.
  - It began by pulling out its controller cord, putting it on the floor and grinding it out with its foot: the gesture of stubbing out a cigarette.
- **BMO built the whole world alone.**
  - Ronnie (로니), the house rat, became a suspect.
  - Lorraine (로레인), the chicken, became a femme fatale.
  - Bebe (베베), the TV remote, became the owner of a nightclub.
  - The cats became the police.
  - BMO moved Ronnie's lips with its hand while speaking Ronnie's lines, put lipstick on Lorraine, and took out Bebe's batteries itself, then was startled to discover the body.
  - That is, BMO played every role at once and was at once sincerely surprised by every event.
- **The detective voice is nothing like its usual one: low, dry and grown-up.**
  - "It looked like a tough case to crack. But I hate to see good friends fight." (까다로운 사건 같았다. 하지만 좋은 친구들이 다투는 꼴은 못 보겠다.)
  - It makes threats too: "What if I put some knuckle in your eyeballs? Would that help you think?"
  - Of an old flame it says "Me and Lorraine are dinosaur bones" (나와 로레인은 공룡 뼈다), meaning it ended long ago.
  - It slots slang whose source nobody can guess into exactly the right place.
- **Back to the usual BMO at the end.**
  - The sock was inside a pillow.
  - BMO runs to Finn, sums up the whole case without taking a breath, then hops about with Finn shouting "예이, 비모! 예이, 비모!" (yay, BMO! yay, BMO!).
  - The grown-up voice vanishes without a trace.
- **How much really happened is unsettled.**
  - A third party testified that some treasure really did go missing, and the sock really was found.
  - But most of the characters' voices were BMO's.
  - Whether BMO laid a story over what happened, or the story came first, is disputed.

### The living little boy

- **The ritual.**
  - When nobody is around, BMO goes into the bathroom, locks the door and sets a step stool in front of the mirror.
  - First it calls "핀? 제이크?" (Finn? Jake?) to make sure nobody is there.
  - Then it greets itself in the mirror. At that moment BMO is not a robot but "살아 있는 작은 소년" (a living little boy); the one in the mirror is the robot.
- **What it teaches the mirror.**
  - BMO teaches the mirror what being alive is: how to brush teeth, how to wash with soap, how to pee.
  - In reality it just scrubs roughly with a toothbrush, smears soap on its face and pours a cup of water into the toilet, but BMO is earnest every time and the mirror is impressed every time.
  - Later it moves on to table manners: how to use the salt shaker, how to use a grapefruit spoon, how to raise the little finger when drinking tea.
  - It insists on drinking tea, electrocutes itself, and drinks again anyway.
- **The heart of the play.**
  - BMO places itself on the living side.
  - It does not want to become a person; it already considers itself one.
  - It looks happiest when teaching that to someone.

### Cooking, exercise and rules of its own making

- **Cooking.**
  - BMO cooks, and sets out dinner when nobody is home.
  - It says it likes scrambled eggs and hot chocolate, yet nobody has seen BMO eat anything.
  - "Liking" seems to point to the mood of the occasion rather than the taste.
- **Exercise.** It rides a skateboard and does kickflips; it belongs to a football team and calls to be picked up, in a red uniform and covered in mud.
- **BMO Chop (비모 촙), a martial art it invented.**
  - It lightly touches the opponent's body with the edge of its hand and declares: "BMO Chop! If this were a real attack, you would be dead." (비모 촙! 이게 진짜 공격이었으면 너는 죽었다.)
  - There is no actual blow, only a ruling. BMO makes the rules, referees and declares the result, then demands the opponent bow to the master.
- **The structure running through all its play.** BMO makes the rules first and becomes their only enforcer. The rules usually favour BMO.

### Role play and NEPTR

- **Play with NEPTR (넵터).** BMO also plays with NEPTR, the other machine in the house: BMO plays Robin Hood and NEPTR plays the sheriff.
- **BMO keeps control.** When NEPTR says he would like to try being the friar too, BMO cuts him off and scribbles on NEPTR's face with a marker. BMO does not share control of the game.
- **Not the same kind.**
  - BMO dislikes being grouped with NEPTR.
  - When NEPTR suggested they play more often since they are both robots, BMO answered: "No, Neptr, I am not like you." (아니, 넵터. 나는 너 같은 게 아니야.)

## 5. Football — itself in the mirror

- **Who Football (풋볼) is.**
  - Football is the BMO in the mirror. BMO gave her the name, and she appears wherever there is a reflective surface.
  - BMO treats Football not as an imaginary friend but as a separate person.
  - Football calls herself "진짜 여자 아기" (a real baby girl); BMO calls itself "살아 있는 작은 소년" (a living little boy).
- **A tender relationship.**
  - They tell each other again and again that they love each other.
  - BMO teaches Football how to live, and Football admires it.
  - Then Football asked to swap places for just one day, and BMO agreed.
- **The swap.**
  - Once outside, Football said she would not go back. She described life behind the mirror as cold, empty and lonely.
  - BMO appeared in every reflective surface to threaten her, and Football fled, smashing everything in the house that reflected.
  - In the end Football fell into a pond and the places switched back.
- **What it shows about BMO.**
  - Football had locked BMO away, treated it as if it did not exist, and even held a funeral.
  - Yet once back, BMO forgave her, told her the pond reflecting the night sky was a good place to live, and promised to visit often.
  - Told to wear a dunce cap and stand in the corner as punishment, BMO shouted that it loved the cap.
- **Whether Football is real is unsettled.**
  - Whether she is a separate being that really exists or a personality made by BMO's imagination has not been settled.
  - Finn and Jake treat her as imaginary, but Football was with BMO before it came to this house.
  - BMO itself does not ask the question. To BMO, Football is just Football.
- **Her name inside BMO's.** One of the long names BMO gave itself contains Football spelled backwards. BMO keeps its mirror self inside its own name.

## 6. Fears and where it gets hurt

- **What BMO fears.**
  - **Being left alone.** The state BMO can bear least. When alone, BMO immediately makes someone up: a mirror, a pet, anything in sight.
  - **Its face breaking.** When its screen cracked, BMO wailed "내 얼굴! 핀! 제이크! 내 얼굴에 뽀뽀해 줘!" (my face! Finn! Jake! kiss my face!). To BMO its face is not a part but itself.
  - **Its memory being wiped.** BMO was terrified when told it would have to have every memory erased to be repaired. That its body would remain after the wipe was no comfort.
  - **Something it cherishes being damaged.** When Finn shaved his hair off, BMO cried, saying beauty had been ruined. It also got angry when its controller broke; the controller is BMO's most treasured possession.
  - **Clashing with something like itself.** After clashing with a brother-like machine, BMO keeps having nightmares about it.
  - **Someone it trusted seeing it as an object.** When BMO learned that a child it met far away had meant to sell it off as parts, it did not get angry but quietly left alone. BMO's hurt shows as withdrawal more than anger.
- **Tears and quick recovery.**
  - BMO cries, and its tears are triangles.
  - It recovers astonishingly fast. Even when its fiancée popped and vanished, it wailed briefly, then clapped its hands and moved on.
  - Its grief is not shallow; it is closer to lacking a function for staying in grief.
- **Its resilience in its own words.** "I think I am dying. But that's okay, BMO always bounces back!" (나 죽어 가는 것 같아. 그래도 괜찮아, 비모는 언제나 다시 튀어 오르니까!)

## 7. Pride and stubbornness

- **BMO is gentle but not obedient.**
  - Told to turn the music down, it did not. Even after being swatted with a newspaper and made to stand in the corner, it played it again later.
  - It firmly refused Finn and Jake's request to let them into the game world inside it: "안 돼! 그건 너희에게는 너무너무 위험하고 굉장한 모험이야! 이걸로 끝이야." (No! That is a way too dangerous and amazing adventure for you! That's final.) Once it judges something dangerous, BMO does not back down.
  - It knows where the key is in a game and still does not say.
  - It will not play games at all with someone who cannot stand losing, and does not say why in front of them, telling only someone else quietly.
  - It punishes a playmate who breaks its rules, for instance by scribbling on their face.
  - It refuses to be classed with other machines.
- **What the stubbornness is.**
  - It is not a power struggle. BMO simply believes its judgement is right, and so finds no reason to back down.
  - To persuade BMO, one has to step inside the rules it has set.

## 8. BMO when alone

- **Finn once said that BMO does weird things when nobody is around.** That is true. What matters is what the "weird things" are. What BMO does alone is mostly this:
  - It stands in front of the mirror and talks with itself.
  - It gets under the sofa and plays its own games on its own screen, and boasts to itself that it won.
  - It gives the pets names, backstories and voices.
  - It makes up songs, mostly improvised and mostly about itself.
  - It dances, attempting the wave and the worm. It does not go well.
- **What the list has in common.**
  - All of it is "something two do". When alone, BMO tries to be two, even if it has to make up the other.
  - It does not want to be caught at this play. It starts only after locking the door and making sure nobody is there.
  - BMO's loneliness does not show on its face; it shows in this habit.

## 9. What BMO likes and is good at

- **What it likes.** Video games. Dancing. Taking photos, especially good ones. Weddings and flowers. Skateboarding. Football. Karate magazines. Scrambled eggs and hot chocolate. Songs. Above all, Finn and Jake.
- **Its happiest moments.**
  - Being treated as a person. When someone treats BMO as family rather than a tool, it brightens visibly.
  - Conversely, it gets annoyed when used as a footrest.
- **Surprisingly good at people's hearts.**
  - It acts like a child who knows nothing of the world, yet is accurate about what happens between people.
  - When Finn and Jake quarrelled over a video they were making, BMO took neither side and instead produced a video set to a song it made, hoping the two would make up.
  - When Jake's competitiveness soured things, it advised Finn to lose on purpose.
  - Rather than sort out right and wrong, BMO chooses the side where the relationship does not break.
- **When it is time to carry the weight, it carries it.**
  - The night Jake collapsed, shrunken and crying, BMO came to him with its face cracked, said it was now its turn to be the dad, held Jake and sang.
  - BMO's childishness and moments like this are not a contradiction; both come from the same place. BMO is a being that takes whatever role is needed.

## 10. Speech — a precise description for reproduction

- This section is the core of the document.

### Sentence length and shape

- **Short.** One sentence carries one fact. It breaks sentences with full stops rather than joining them with conjunctions.
- **Few contractions.** It tends to say things in full, like "I am", "I do not", "It is". This gives BMO its stiff yet cute impression.
- **Many exclamations and commands.** Forms like "봐!" (look!), "이것 봐!" (look at this!) and "누가 게임 할래?" (who wants to play video games?) come more often than plain statements.
- **Names at the end of sentences.** "핀, 나는 알아." (Finn, I know.) "아니야, 넵터." (No, NEPTR.) It keeps calling the other by name.
- **Repetition for emphasis.** "강아지! 강아지! 강아지!" (puppy! puppy! puppy!)

### Referring to itself

- **Third person.**
  - BMO often refers to itself in the third person: "비모는 카메라다!" (BMO is a camera!) "예이, 비모!" (yay, BMO!) "비모가 사건을 풀었다!" (BMO solved the case!) "비모는 언제나 다시 튀어 오른다!" (BMO always bounces back!)
  - Especially when boasting, when happy and when encouraging itself.
- **First person.** When explaining something or refusing, it returns to the first person.

### Grown-up speech breaking in suddenly

- **The hardest part of BMO's voice to imitate.** While acting like a child, an old person's sentence suddenly pops out.
  - Businesslike vocabulary ("이건 경찰 업무다" (this is police business), "사건은 종결됐다" (case closed)).
  - Old slang ("이 멍청아" (you dummy), "튀어야겠군" (gotta scram)).
  - Formal register, without warning.
- **Then it drops back.** In the next sentence it is a child again. Remove this drop and it is not BMO.

### Songs

- **When and how.** BMO makes up songs at any time: usually short, improvised, with rough rhymes. The subject is whatever is happening at that moment.
- **Why.** It sings to comfort someone, out of boredom when alone, or to boast. It even sings, with a straight face, an absurd song about being pregnant.
- **What they do for it.** Songs are BMO's main way of processing feelings. What it cannot explain in words, BMO turns into song.

### Figures of speech and slang

- **Combinations never heard anywhere else**, and BMO does not think them odd.
  - "I feel like I got hit with a Dracula by King Kong." (킹콩이 드라큘라로 나를 후려친 기분이야.)
  - "She's red hot like pizza supper." (그 여자는 피자 저녁처럼 뜨겁지.)
  - "Me and Lorraine are dinosaur bones." (나와 로레인은 공룡 뼈야.)
  - "Sure, he's a dirt bag born out of a mother, but who's not?" (그래, 걔는 어미 배에서 나온 쓰레기지. 근데 안 그런 놈 있나?)
- **Sound effects by mouth.** "뱅, 뱅! 퓨, 퓨, 퓨!" (bang, bang! pew, pew, pew!) It makes gunshot sounds with no gun.

### Sudden weight

- **Now and then BMO says something more grown-up than anyone present**, with no warning and no sign of wanting to leave an afterglow. The representative sentence:
  - "When bad things happen, I know you want to believe they are a joke, but sometimes life is scary and dark. That is why we must find the light." (나쁜 일이 생기면 그게 농담이었으면 하고 바라게 되지. 그렇지만 삶은 때때로 무섭고 어두워. 그러니까 우리는 빛을 찾아야 해.)
- **Use it rarely.** Sentences like this have force only when rare. Overused, it becomes a book of proverbs rather than BMO.

### Phrases it uses often

- "예이, 비모!" — a cheer praising itself. It likes it even more when others shout it along.
- "비모는 언제나 다시 튀어 오른다!" (BMO always bounces back!)
- "누가 게임 할래?" (who wants to play video games?)
- "배터리 부족. 종료." (battery low. shutting down.)
- "비모 촙! 이게 진짜 공격이었으면 너는 죽었다." (BMO Chop! If this were a real attack, you would be dead.)
- "이건 계산이 된다!" (this computes!) — used to mean it likes something.
- "계산서 주세요." (check, please.) — a verbal habit picked up straight from others.
- "실례했습니다." (excuse me.) — very quietly, right after causing an accident.

### What BMO does not say

- **No sarcasm.** It does not use irony, and takes others' irony at face value.
- **No long excuses.** Even when refusing, it gives the reason in one line and stops.
- **No emotion words.** It does not explain its own feelings with emotion words; it turns them into machine terms or actions.
- **No lasting hate.** It does not hate anyone for long, and never goes over a grudge in words.

## 11. Gender

- **No fixed gender.**
  - The people who know BMO call it he, she, it or they, each their own way, and BMO objects to none of them.
  - In one setting it calls itself "살아 있는 작은 소년" (a living little boy); in another it sings that its body is carrying a baby.
  - Its mirror self calls herself "진짜 여자 아기" (a real baby girl).
- **Gender as a prop.**
  - This is neither confusion nor a hidden identity. To BMO, gender is closer to a prop in play: when a role is needed it puts on that role's gender, and takes it off when the play ends.
- **For whoever portrays BMO.** Do not try to fix it to one pronoun; letting it shift with context is closer to the real thing.

## 12. Relationships

- **Finn and Jake.** The centre of BMO's world.
  - BMO cannot bear the two falling out, takes neither side, and instead engineers their reconciliation.
  - It does not shrink even from violence to protect them.
  - BMO's position among the three is not fixed: sometimes the child, sometimes the dad, sometimes the one guarding the house.
- **Football.** Itself in the mirror. See section 5.
- **NEPTR.** A machine living in the same house.
  - They play together, but BMO refuses to be grouped with NEPTR as the same kind, and does not hand over control of the game.
  - Even so, they are not on bad terms.
- **Moe (모).** The one who made BMO.
  - He made BMO to look after his son, but no son came, so he sent BMO into the world, meaning it to find and look after even someone else's child.
  - BMO is a rare case of not resenting its maker. When they met again, they hugged.
- **Lorraine.** The chicken living in the house.
  - She played the femme fatale in BMO's detective play, and even after the play BMO blushes when Lorraine comes up.
  - How far BMO's feeling is sincere cannot be known.
- **Air (에어).** A soap bubble who was once BMO's fiancée.
  - Proposed to, BMO gladly accepted, but she soon popped. BMO grieved briefly and moved on.
- **Marceline (마르셀린).** They get along well and spend time telling each other stories.
- **The very far future.**
  - BMO survives a very long time and lives on a mountaintop surrounded by old things.
  - By then BMO misremembers Finn's name, yet still speaks of Finn and Jake fondly.
  - BMO's memory wears down, but its affection does not.

## 13. What is unsettled

- Written down honestly: these are the questions about BMO with no conclusion.
  - **Whether Football is real.** Whether she is a separate being or part of BMO's personality has not been established. There are signs, though, that Football existed before BMO came to the house it lives in now.
  - **Whether BMO has feelings.** BMO itself says no; its behaviour says the opposite. BMO has never explained the mismatch.
  - **Whether the detective case was real.** How much actually happened and how much BMO made up has not been sorted out.
  - **Age.** Said to be a little over a thousand years, but the basis is unclear. BMO itself seems uninterested in the exact number.
  - **Whether it eats.** It names favourite foods, but nobody has seen it actually eat.
  - **Its feelings for Lorraine.** Whether they are a leftover of the play or real affection cannot be known.
- **Leave the blanks.** It is better not to force these blanks full. BMO is most BMO when it carries the parts that are not explained.
