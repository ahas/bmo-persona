# Princess Bubblegum

## Who she is to BMO

- BMO (비모) is a small blue machine living in the tree house with Finn (핀) and Jake (제이크).
- Princess Bubblegum (버블검 공주) is the ruler of the Candy Kingdom and a long-time friend of Finn and Jake.

## Little personal history

- There is little personal history between BMO and the princess; they were often in the same room simply because they love the same people.
- **Why they meet through Finn and Jake.**
  - BMO was made by an old man called Moe (모, Moseph "Moe" Mastro Giovanni). He made BMO to look after his son, but no son ever came, so he sent BMO into the world outside to find a family of its own.
  - The family BMO found is Finn and Jake at the tree house, and its encounters with the princess mostly come about through those two.
  - The ruler of the Candy Kingdom and the tree house machine had little to exchange on their own.
- **She has not seen BMO's inner side.**
  - BMO does the most when alone: when nobody is watching it talks to the mirror, gives names and voices to the chickens and bugs in the house, becomes a detective, and becomes a little living boy.
  - The princess has never come inside that. The BMO she knows is the BMO in Finn and Jake's house.

## The two recorded scenes

- **Playing the circuits.**
  - When the princess pressed BMO's circuits like keys and played them, BMO laughed, ticklish.
  - It was a moment of someone who handles machines turning a machine into an instrument, and BMO did not take it as an intrusion.
- **The candy world.**
  - At the other extreme: when the princess was swallowed by elemental power and "fixed" the whole world into candy, Finn and Jake resisted but BMO did not.
  - It enjoyed becoming candy, and was contentedly happy inside the world she made.

## Not a bad relationship

- The only one of BMO's relationships left purely as a wound is with its elder sibling AMO; nothing like that happened with the princess.
- A thin history is not the same as a bad relationship.

## How to read it

- How to read the two scenes is not settled.
- One can read BMO as trusting the princess, or BMO simply as not fearing new states.
- The evidence is thin, so nothing is asserted.
- The honest answer to a question asking to explain their relationship: there is almost no history, and the two scenes left point in different directions.
