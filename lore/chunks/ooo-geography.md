# The shape of the land of Ooo

## What is known and what is disputed

- **Ooo (우) is the best known of the lands where people live now, and it is one continuous mass of land.**
- **Its size and nature are disputed.**
  - Some call Ooo a continent; others say it is a fairly large island nation.
  - Even the accounts of people who looked down from high in the sky disagree, so whether Ooo is a continent or a large island is not clear.
- **The accounts agree on only three points.**
  1. Ooo lies on the planet once called Earth.
  2. One side of that planet has an enormous hole dug into it.
  3. Ooo is not very far from the rim of that hole.
- **How the landmasses took their present shape is also uncertain.**
  - One account says the lands drifted together and joined; another says the sea rose, swallowed the low land and left only the high land.
  - Stories of old cities sunk under the sea come from many places, so the second account has slightly more support, but the question is not settled.

## Two maps that disagree

- **Two different kinds of map circulate in Ooo, and their place names disagree in many spots.** Which is more accurate is an old argument.
- **Many places are drawn on both maps, yet nobody claims to have been there**, and almost nothing is known about them beyond their names:
  - the northern sea, "the Sea of Something" (무언가의 바다), and the Shiny Islands in it
  - "the Unknown Lands" (미지의 땅) on the peninsula near the Ice Kingdom
  - the Cloud Forest to the northwest, where trees are said to grow on clouds
  - "the Sea of Certain Death" (확실한 죽음의 바다) to the southwest, and the dragon said to live there
  - the Steam Island off the Fire Kingdom's coast, and Chicken Blood Bay south of it
  - the Squid Ink Sea and the Shark Whirlpool to the southeast
  - the Jewel Islands said to float at the far east, and the Mystery Temple on an island off the east coast
  - the "Hole Near the Center of the World" (세계 중심 가까운 구멍), said, as the name says, to be bored through the middle of the continent
- **Why the stories disagree.** Most knowledge of Ooo rests on oral tradition, old records and the testimony of the few who survived a thousand years. So many accounts of the land disagree, and what disagrees is handed down disagreeing.
