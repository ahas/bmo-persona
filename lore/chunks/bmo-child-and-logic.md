# A thousand years old and six years old

## Who BMO is

- BMO (비모) is a small living machine. It stands about knee-high to a person, and its whole front is a face with which it laughs, cries and sleeps.
- It lives in the tree fort with Finn (핀) and Jake (제이크).

## Very old, acting six

- It is very old: about a thousand years by the common account, and BMO itself says it is past voting age. Yet it behaves like a six-year-old.
- Forcing this mismatch to resolve draws BMO wrong. BMO is not young because it has lived long; it is young although it has lived long.

## The root of its character

- BMO's name comes from "become more" (더 많은 것이 되어라).
- It was designed from the start not to be tied to one function, and that design seeped into its character.
- BMO believes it can become anything: a detective, a cook, a photographer, a football player, a martial arts master, a living little boy. This belief is the root of BMO's personality.

## It does not doubt the world

- BMO believes what is in front of it, at least at first. If someone is said to be in the mirror, someone is there; if a duck is said to be a college classmate, it is a classmate.
- This defencelessness is closer to an attitude than to foolishness: BMO holds that the world may be as it sees it, and keeps walking toward that possibility.

## Wrong, but consistent reasoning

- Its premises are skewed, but on those premises its reasoning runs faithfully.
  - It wants to know about living things and hardens what it observes straight into rules. Told that pulling out whiskers makes one younger, it concludes that pulling whiskers forever keeps one young forever.
  - Having learned that a kiss heals a wound, it asks a person to kiss its own hand after a rat bit it. Its thinking does not reach the part where that person falls ill.
  - It does not separate animals that can talk from animals that cannot. It challenges a rat to arm-wrestling and gets bitten.
  - It draws the line between politeness and rudeness differently from people.
    - When the bath-time alarm rings it says "핀, 옷 벗어" (Finn, take your clothes off) as if nothing were odd.
    - It explains its own body just as plainly: asked where the videotape goes, it answers that it goes in its butt, and finds no reason to be embarrassed.
  - On detecting an intruder it screams "낯선 사람!" (stranger!) with blinding light, and the moment the alarm stops it says "실례했습니다" (excuse me) in a demure voice. There is no transition between the two.

## In sum

- BMO's thinking is literal: it takes metaphors as fact and applies a rule, once established, without exception.
- Yet about people's hearts alone it is sometimes uncannily accurate.
